Fit for your frame

  • Tailored, not tight. Ask for "slim-straight" or "tailored" cuts. Fabric should skim the chest and drape at the waist without pulling at buttons.
  • Shirt length ends mid-fly when untucked. Longer reads sloppy, shorter shows a gap.
  • Trousers with little or no break. Hem so the fabric just kisses the shoe. Straight or gently tapered legs balance your shoulders.
  • Jackets end around mid-seat with shoulder seams sitting on the shoulder point.

Your features are the accessories

  • Gray beard + dark round frames love warm mid-tones: tobacco, rust, olive, camel. They make the gray look silver instead of washed out.
  • Avoid head-to-toe black. Charcoal and navy give the same sharpness without draining your skin.
  • Roll sleeves two folds to just below the elbow. It shows the forearm tattoos and looks deliberate.
  • Match leathers loosely: belt and shoes in the same family (all browns), not identical.

The three-color rule

  • One base, one support, one accent. Example: navy base, gray support, rust accent.
  • Tonal works when textures change: gray flannel trousers, gray merino, gray suede.
  • Pattern once. One plaid or stripe per outfit; everything else solid and pulled from the pattern.

Texture does the heavy lifting

  • Neutral outfits need contrast in surface: suede with denim, waffle knit with twill, corduroy with merino.
  • Fabric weight matches Austin weather. Linen and 5 to 7 oz cotton May through September; flannel, wool and suede November through February.
  • Matte beats shiny for everyday wear. Save sheen for evening.
